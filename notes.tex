\subsection{compare to baseline (simple) old fragmenters}
Manuel suggested: Magma, but: 

Short answer: **No — $MAGMa^+$ can’t *predict* a spectrum from a SMILES.** It’s an **annotation tool**: you give it a spectrum (MS/MS or $MS^n$) and a candidate structure, and it maps **substructures** of that structure to the **observed peaks** and scores candidates. It does *not* generate m/z peaks or intensities on its own. ([PMC][1], [Netherlands eScience Center][2], [RSC Publishing][3], [J-STAGE][4])

If you want a “possible spectrum from SMILES” and still use MAGMa in the loop, do this two-step workaround:

1. **Predict a spectrum from the SMILES** with a generator (e.g., **CFM-ID** for ESI MS/MS, or **NEIMS** for EI GC-MS). You’ll get m/z–intensity peaks. ([BioMed Central][5])
2. **Feed that predicted peak list to MAGMa** (as a mass-tree) together with your SMILES in the structure DB. MAGMa will then **annotate** the predicted peaks with fragment **substructures** (useful to inspect plausible fragments), but the peak generation still comes from step 1. ([Netherlands eScience Center][2])
